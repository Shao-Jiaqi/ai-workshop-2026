AI Tools for Business Research

Two days of hands-on training in which you build your own AI research ecosystem — and leave with a research companion that keeps learning from your work.

4 & 5 August 2026 · 09:00–16:45 SGT · Biz 2, Level 5, Seminar Room 05-11 · Bring your laptop

Introduction

Artificial intelligence is opening the door to a new era of business scholarship: one in which every researcher can be accompanied by a personalised team of intelligent agents.

Organised by the Dean's PhD and Research Office, this intensive two-day course is designed to help NUS Business School researchers step confidently into this new world. Through immersive, hands-on training, participants will build their own AI research ecosystem and discover how agentic AI can transform every stage of the research pipeline.

Rather than mastering a single tool, participants will learn how to cultivate an evolving research companion that learns from their daily work and grows alongside the rapidly advancing frontier of agentic AI.

What you will be able to do afterwards

Four capabilities, each practised on your own machine during the two days.

  • Operate Run an agentic coding assistant fluently — install it, manage permissions and cost, and steer it with plan mode and prompts that actually land.
  • Apply Put it on real research chores: exploratory analysis, building a dataset from raw sources such as SEC EDGAR, debugging, and refactoring.
  • Personalise Shape the setup around your own work with custom skills, subagents, and MCP integrations — and wire the agent into an Obsidian knowledge base that compounds.
  • Evaluate Use LLMs across the research pipeline — literature review, data collection, identification — while reasoning about bias, reproducibility, and research ethics.

Prerequisites

The workshop is open to graduate researchers in any business discipline — Accounting, Finance, Marketing, Management, Operations, Information Systems, Economics.

No prior programming experience is required. The second half of Session 1 is a from-zero primer: basic coding literacy, an intuition-level introduction to how these models work, and a guided check of your environment. If you already work in Python, R, or Stata, treat that segment as a setup-verification pass.

What we do assume

  • Comfort working with research data — spreadsheets, CSVs, regressions.
  • No machine-learning theory.
  • A laptop you can install software on, and two accounts: a Claude Code subscription (or API access) and a GitHub account.
  • A free Obsidian install for Session 3A. We build the vault together in class.

Install before you arrive. The two days are compressed, and a room full of people installing software costs everyone an hour. Follow the setup guide — 30 to 45 minutes — and we verify it together in Session 1B rather than building it from scratch.

The two days

Twelve teaching hours. Each session pairs a short conceptual opening with a practice you run on your own laptop. Session 1B, 2, and 3A each end in a hands-on block; the same paper runs through all three, so the work compounds.

Running order is fixed. Clock times are indicative and confirmed on the day.

    The long arc from hand-written code, through the machine-learning turn, to programming in natural language. Then where models fail: bias, hallucination, overconfidence. Fabricated citations, non-reproducibility, data leakage, and the disclosure policies now in force at AEA, Elsevier, and Management Science.

Day two — Wednesday 5 August

  1. 09:0090 minJiaqi Shao

    3A · Obsidian + Claude Code: knowledge management for AI research

    Turning the assistant into a librarian for what you know. A plain-Markdown vault you own and can version with Git; wiring the agent into it so it can read, search, and update notes in place; building an LLM wiki of cross-linked atomic notes; and using the vault as ground truth so answers stay anchored.

    Practice C · make the paper compound
  2. 10:3020 min

    Break

  3. 10:5090 minShirley Wang

    3B · AI for literature review and research ideation

    Opens with an LLM primer — tokens, embeddings, the context window, prompt versus fine-tune, and text as data: defining a construct, scoring text against it, validating against a hand-coded sample. Then searching and synthesising a literature, positioning a question, and the failure mode that matters most — hallucinated and miscited references.

  4. 12:2070 min

    Lunch

  5. 13:3090 minShirley Wang

    4A · Data collection and alternative data

    Turning messy public sources into a dataset you can defend. Agentic collection of filings, transcripts, and documents at scale; extracting variables and linking to standard identifiers, always checked against a hand-validated sample; what satellite, geospatial, and audio data add and where they mislead; and the codebook-and-validation discipline that lets a replication team trust what you built.

  6. 15:0010 min

    Break

  7. 15:1090 minShirley Wang

    4B · Identification and research design

    From a dataset to a credible causal claim, with the agent as implementation partner: you supply the design, it supplies the code. Fixed effects and clustering with a full audit trail; difference-in-differences and its modern variants, event studies and placebo tests; what randomisation buys; and the spurious-findings trap that arrives when you turn AI on the design itself.

How the workshop runs

This is a short course, not a for-credit course: no grades, no problem-set marks, no final project. What you get out of it is proportional to what you build during and between the sessions.

  • Bring your laptop to both days and follow along live.
  • Finish the setup before Tuesday morning.
  • Bring a small piece of your own data, or one research chore you would like to automate. The practices work far better on a problem you actually care about.
  • Leaning on the assistant heavily is the entire point — while learning to verify what it hands back.
